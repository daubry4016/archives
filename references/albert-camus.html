ALBERT CAMUS

1913 — 1960
writer · philosopher · journalist

biography

Albert Camus was a French-Algerian writer, philosopher and journalist whose work examined the conflict between humanity's desire for meaning and a world that offers no guaranteed answer.

His writing developed the idea of the absurd as a condition arising from this confrontation. Rather than resolving the contradiction through certainty, Camus explored rebellion, freedom and the possibility of living fully despite the absence of absolute meaning.

works

The Stranger
A novel concerning alienation, social expectations, mortality and the strange distance between individual experience and the world around it.
The Myth of Sisyphus
A philosophical essay examining the absurd and asking how one might live when existence offers no final answer.
The Plague
A novel about suffering, solidarity, responsibility and resistance in the face of an indifferent reality.
The Rebel
An examination of rebellion, revolution, violence and the moral limits of attempts to transform society.
