ARTHUR SCHOPENHAUER

1788 — 1860
philosopher · writer

biography

Arthur Schopenhauer was a German philosopher whose work developed a deeply pessimistic account of existence, placing the concept of will at the center of his philosophy.

Influenced in part by Kant and by Indian philosophical traditions, Schopenhauer argued that human beings are driven by a restless will that continually produces desire and dissatisfaction. He nevertheless regarded art, compassion and the temporary suspension of desire as possible forms of relief.

works

The World as Will and Representation
His principal philosophical work, presenting the world as both representation and an underlying striving will.
On the Basis of Morality
A work examining the foundations of ethical behavior and emphasizing compassion as a basis for morality.
Essays and Aphorisms
Shorter philosophical writings concerning human nature, happiness, knowledge, art and the difficulties of life.
Parerga and Paralipomena
A collection of essays and reflections covering a wide range of philosophical and cultural subjects.

related

Friedrich Nietzsche
Nietzsche was strongly influenced by Schopenhauer early in his intellectual development, before eventually rejecting important parts of his pessimistic philosophy.
Immanuel Kant
Schopenhauer's distinction between the world as it appears and its underlying reality developed partly from his interpretation of Kant.
Buddhism & Hindu Philosophy
Schopenhauer drew inspiration from Indian philosophical traditions, particularly ideas concerning desire, suffering and liberation.
Suffering
For Schopenhauer, suffering is deeply connected to the endless cycle of wanting, striving and dissatisfaction.
