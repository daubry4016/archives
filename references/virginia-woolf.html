VIRGINIA WOOLF

1882 — 1941
novelist · essayist · modernist

biography

Virginia Woolf was an English writer and one of the central figures of literary modernism. Her work transformed the representation of consciousness, memory and ordinary experience in fiction.

Rather than treating inner life as something separate from the outside world, Woolf's writing moves continuously between thought, sensation, memory and perception. Time becomes fluid, and seemingly ordinary moments acquire an unusual depth.

works

Mrs Dalloway
A single day unfolding through memory, consciousness, social connection and the passage of time.
To the Lighthouse
A meditation on family, perception, absence, memory and the changing nature of time.
A Room of One's Own
An extended essay concerning women, literature, independence, education and the conditions required for creative work.
The Waves
A highly experimental novel constructed through interconnected voices, exploring identity, time and consciousness.
