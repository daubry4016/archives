FRANZ KAFKA

1883 — 1924
writer · novelist · observer of the absurd

biography

Franz Kafka was a German-speaking Jewish writer from Prague whose fiction became known for its unsettling treatment of alienation, bureaucracy, guilt and power.

Much of his work places ordinary individuals inside strange and incomprehensible systems. The resulting worlds are simultaneously familiar and impossible, where rules exist without explanation and escape often seems perpetually out of reach.

works

The Metamorphosis
A story of isolation, identity and family after an inexplicable transformation disrupts an ordinary life.
The Trial
A surreal encounter with an inaccessible legal system, where accusation exists without a clear explanation.
The Castle
An unfinished novel centered on an outsider attempting to reach an elusive authority that remains distant and incomprehensible.
The Judgment
A psychologically intense story concerning authority, guilt, family and judgment.

related

Absurdism
Kafka's worlds frequently place individuals inside situations that resist rational explanation, making his work closely associated with the experience of the absurd.
Existentialism
Questions of isolation, freedom, responsibility and existence emerge repeatedly throughout Kafka's fiction.
The Unfinished
Several of Kafka's major works remained unfinished, leaving stories suspended between revelation and incompletion.
