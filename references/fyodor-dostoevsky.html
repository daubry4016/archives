FYODOR DOSTOEVSKY

1821 — 1881
novelist · philosopher · observer of human nature

biography

Fyodor Dostoevsky was a Russian novelist whose works explored morality, faith, guilt, freedom and the contradictions within human consciousness.

His life was marked by political persecution, imprisonment and profound personal and intellectual upheaval. These experiences became inseparable from the questions that shaped his fiction: what makes an action moral, whether freedom is possible without responsibility, and how a person might live in the presence of suffering and uncertainty.

works

Crime and Punishment
A psychological novel concerning guilt, morality, isolation and the consequences of transgression.
The Brothers Karamazov
A wide-ranging exploration of faith, doubt, morality, freedom, family and the problem of suffering.
Notes from Underground
A fragmented and confrontational examination of consciousness, rationality, freedom and alienation.
The Idiot
A novel exploring innocence, compassion, society, morality and the difficulty of remaining good in a contradictory world.

related

Existentialism
Dostoevsky's explorations of freedom, responsibility, isolation and meaning became deeply influential to later existentialist thinkers.
Nihilism
His fiction repeatedly confronts the possibility of a world without stable moral foundations and examines what follows from that possibility.
Friedrich Nietzsche
Nietzsche regarded Dostoevsky as an important psychological observer, and both writers explored morality, suffering, freedom and the limits of human self-understanding.
The Underground
A recurring image of isolation, contradiction and consciousness turned inward.
