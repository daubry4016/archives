EDGAR ALLAN POE

1809 — 1849
poet · writer · literary critic

biography

Edgar Allan Poe was an American poet, writer and literary critic whose work became closely associated with mystery, psychological horror and the darker possibilities of the imagination.

His writing frequently explores grief, obsession, memory, mortality and the instability of perception. He also played an important role in the development of the modern detective story and wrote extensively about the principles of literary composition and artistic effect.

works

The Raven
A poem shaped by grief, memory and the obsessive return of a voice that refuses to disappear.
The Tell-Tale Heart
A psychological tale concerning guilt, perception and the instability of a narrator's own mind.
The Fall of the House of Usher
A gothic story of decay, isolation, family and the unsettling relationship between place and mind.
The Murders in the Rue Morgue
A foundational detective story introducing C. Auguste Dupin and emphasizing reason, observation and deduction.
